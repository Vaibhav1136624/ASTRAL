FIELD NOTES

Give light a way through.

01 / Pick up and placeDrag a prism, green filter, lens or mirror from the limited tray into the chamber. You can also click a tray slot, then click where it should go. Drag an optic back to the tray or use Return to recover it. Double-click an optic to pick it up; click to place, or Escape to cancel.

02 / Find the spectrumThe crescent emits white light. A prism separates it into a continuous rainbow: violet and blue bend more than red. The green filter reflects only 510–550 nm and transmits the remaining bands. Place and rotate it within the green portion of the spectrum. Green, blue, red and yellow receivers each need their matching wavelength.

03 / Reflect and focusMirrors reflect every wavelength that reaches their surface. Lenses focus passing rays. Rotate the selected optic using its gold handle, the angle slider, or arrow keys. Alt + arrow rotates by 0.1°. Shift + arrow moves it by two units. A hint shows one intended position and angle; other solutions are welcome.

04 / Cross dimensionsEvery tenth level enters curved space through the portal. Mirrors briefly stabilize rays along their normal; lenses soften curvature. Later dimensions reverse gravity at the marked boundary. Every fifth completed level reveals a cosmos field note.

05 / Increasing difficultyLater chambers require longer reflection chains, tighter wavelength matching, smaller receivers, and narrow passages through physical walls. Receiver shields leave a single entrance for the intended beam. The gold-tipped wall openings are the routes through the chamber.

06 / Listen to the lightWith sound enabled, placing an object in a beam plays a soft tone mapped from the intercepted wavelengths. Shorter wavelengths map to higher audible notes. Rotation produces a quiet mechanical click. Empty placements and idle scenes stay silent.

This is a stylized optical puzzle: prism dispersion is enlarged for readability, lenses use a focusing approximation, and wavelengths are mapped to musical tones—not literal sounds emitted by light. About visible light · NASA ↗
